  - type: faq-group
    id: a
    nav_label: Before you get in touch
    heading: Before you get in touch.
    items:
      - id: a1
        question: What should I send in the first message?
        answer: |-
          A short, neutral message through WhatsApp or Signal, such as: "I'd like a private conversation. Please let me know the next step." You receive a personal reply as soon as possible. Do not send passwords, recovery codes, screenshots, legal documents, intimate material or details, or detailed allegations in that first message. Keep identifying case details out of it as well.
      - id: a2
        question: Can someone notice my contact with Toza?
        answer: |-
          Contact through WhatsApp or Signal is not guaranteed to be invisible on your device or account. Keep the first message short and neutral.
      - id: a3
        question: Is the first conversation the paid service?
        answer: |-
          No. The first conversation checks whether Toza fits your situation, whether anything appears urgent and what the next step should be. It is free and includes no device inspection, mapping, diagnosis or technical work. Contacting Toza does not commit you to a purchase.
      - id: a4
        question: What happens before I pay?
        answer: |-
          {shared:common.written_offer} You accept it explicitly and pay; then the visit is scheduled. {shared:common.paid_after} Preparation instructions are sent directly.
      - id: a5
        question: Do I need to know what's wrong before I get in touch?
        answer: |-
          No. You can have a concern, a hunch, or a wish to take a more deliberate approach to your digital life. You do not need to identify an account, setting or technical fault. The first conversation checks whether Toza fits; examining your digital environment belongs to the first step. A concern alone does not establish that someone has accessed your information.

      - id: b4
        question: What if you find something serious outside my concern?
        answer: |-
          Toza explains it and obtains your approval before addressing it. If the proposed work expands the scope or price, that is agreed with you first. Finding an urgent problem does not authorize unagreed work or charges.
      - id: b5
        question: Can you remove spyware or a malicious app?
        answer: |-
          Where it can be done safely within the agreed scope, yes. Urgent first aid during the first step can include removing a malicious app or profile, with your approval. Toza explains each material change before acting. Removing it does not prove who installed it or how.

  - type: faq-group

    nav_label: Privacy and control
    heading: Privacy and control.
    items:
      - id: d1
        question: Do I give you my passwords or remote access?
        answer: |-
          {shared:common.your_credentials} Do not send them in advance. {shared:common.sign_in} Toza does not keep your credentials for ongoing use or remotely control your devices.
      - id: d2
        question: Will you see my messages or photos?
        answer: |-
          Some information may be visible on your own screen where it is necessary for the agreed work, with you present. Toza explains the relevant access and agrees with you what needs to be reviewed. The service is not a general inspection of your private content.
      - id: d3
        question: Do you keep records or use the cloud?
        answer: |-
          Your situation and the information you share are handled confidentially. Your accounts and shared services may themselves use cloud systems. During the core engagement, Toza does not upload a client dataset to a Toza cloud-analysis platform or retain a copy or comprehensive log of your digital life beyond agreed deliverables and ordinary lawful business records. This does not mean that no cloud services or administrative records exist.
      - id: d4
        question: Do you monitor my devices or install software?
        answer: |-
          No. Toza does not monitor your devices or install security software on them. The work focuses on the settings and access arrangements within your authorized environment. Every protection tier also includes practical security habits and personal teaching so you understand how to use the resulting setup.

  - type: faq-group
    id: e
    nav_label: What Toza does not do
    heading: What Toza does not do.
    items:
      - id: e1
        question: Can you prove who accessed my account, investigate someone or collect evidence?
        answer: |-
          No. Toza examines your own authorized digital environment and reduces the exposure identified within the agreed scope. It does not investigate another person, attribute past activity, reconstruct events forensically, collect court evidence or give legal advice. Finding a current access path does not prove who used it or how information reached someone else. Changing access cannot recall copies already held elsewhere.
      - id: e2
        question: Will the other side notice the changes?
        answer: |-
          Some changes are visible. Ending a signed-in session, for example, signs that device out, and some changes create notifications for other users. Toza explains material changes and agrees their timing and sequence with you before acting. It does not promise that changes will go unnoticed.
      - id: e3
        question: Will this escalate my dispute? Is it legal?
        answer: |-
          Toza works only within your own authorized digital environment, on access you are authorized to manage. Changing recovery details or ending shared access can interrupt access or create notifications, so the effects are explained before you approve them. With your permission, timing can be coordinated with your attorney. Legal strategy stays with your attorney; Toza does not give legal advice.
      - id: e4
        question: Can you work on company systems or a relative's account?
        answer: |-
          Only where you have lawful access and agreed authority. A dispute does not itself authorize changes to company systems or another person's accounts. A family connection does not authorize work on someone else's account or device. Toza does not determine who is entitled to an account, document or asset, and is not a corporate security audit.
      - id: e5
        question: Is the dated record a certification or a security guarantee?
        answer: |-
          No. It is a practical, dated account of the work performed, not a forensic report, legal opinion, court-evidence document or certification. A technically blocked or declined action is not presented as a completed change. The record does not guarantee that every possible exposure has been found or removed.

    heading: For attorneys.
    items:
      - id: f1
        question: Does a referral authorize access to my client's information?
        answer: |-
          No. A referral does not authorize access to devices, accounts or findings. The client must authorize the work and have lawful authority over the environment being reviewed. Coordination with counsel requires the client's permission; the referring attorney is not automatically a recipient of personal information or findings.
      - id: f2
        question: What does the attorney-friendly summary contain, and who decides?
        answer: |-
          With the client's explicit authorization, an attorney-friendly summary can describe the work and remaining issues. The client decides what may be disclosed. It is a practical account of the work, not legal advice or a forensic report, and it does not establish what another person did.
      - id: f3
        question: Can timing be coordinated with proceedings?
        answer: |-
          Yes, with the client's permission. Timing of changes can be coordinated with counsel around a filing, a negotiation or a hearing. Legal strategy remains with the attorney; the client authorizes technical changes.
      - id: f4
        question: How does referral work commercially?
        answer: |-
          A referral fee is paid when a referred client's first step is paid, and a further fee if the client continues to a protection tier. Amounts and terms are in a one-page agreement.
