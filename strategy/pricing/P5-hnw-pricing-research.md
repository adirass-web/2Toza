# P5 — How white-glove services price to HNW clients, and what it means for Toza

Date: 2026-10-15. Scope: price structures and psychology outside P1/1a (not repeated: BlackCloak, Aprivé, DeleteMe/Incogni, PartnerMD basics, Israeli freelancer rates, JPMorgan Advice Lab). Figures are as published by third parties, mostly secondary web pages; USD unless stated. "(my calc)" = my arithmetic or judgement. "Unverified" = single, secondary or promotional source. FX used for rough conversion: about ₪3.5 per $ (my calc; check live rate).

## Summary: 10 findings

1. **Top tiers sell fewer clients per expert, not more features.** Executive concierge medicine is reported at roughly $6,000–25,000+ a year with panels sometimes under 100 patients, against $2,000–6,000 for standard concierge [1]. Small panel size is the product.
2. **The diagnostic is priced as its own premium event.** PartnerMD lists an annual executive physical at $2,700–4,000 and physical plus membership at $5,300–7,600 per person [2]. The one-off, named, in-person assessment is a normal HNW category.
3. **Every comparable fixed-fee professional ladder is tiered by client complexity, with an open top.** A US prenup flat-fee guide runs $1,500–2,500 up to $15,000–25,000+ for ultra-high-net-worth cases, driven by asset type, entities, jurisdictions and likely pushback [3]. Toza's household size and device/account count play the same role.
4. **When unsure between tiers, quote the higher one.** The same guide advises it, or a small paid preliminary review ($500–750) before a full quote [3]. That is Toza's paid Assessment, sized differently.
5. **Rush and short-notice premiums are normal.** Reported surcharges of 25% (under 60 days) and 50% (under 30 days) [3]. A defined urgent-response premium fits Toza's divorce/dispute clientele.
6. **Protection and investigation anchors are hourly or daily and large.** Executive protection is cited at about $125–250 per hour per agent and $3,000–5,000 a day for a two-person team [4]. A single-agent detail runs about $36,000–75,000 a month [5]. Toza's ₪14,000 is small next to a few days of these services (my calc).
7. **Private investigators work on deposit retainers.** Typical hourly $50–250, upfront deposits of $500–10,000 or more, and narrow tasks such as sweeps for surveillance devices sold as flat fees [6]. This is the cheap end of discreet investigative work, and the closest low anchor for "checking a device".
8. **High price is read as quality only when tied to a story.** Prestige-price commentary says a premium works through scarcity, craft or provenance, and without it reads as a "brand tax" [7]. Luxury practitioners add that inconsistent prices and unpredictable discounts erode trust [8].
9. **Round prices suit emotion-led purchases.** A peer-reviewed study (Wadhwa and Zhang, 2015) found rounded prices raise purchase likelihood when the decision is feelings-led, and non-round when it is practical [9]. Toza's buyers are fear- and relief-led; round figures fit.
10. **Thresholds are published as filters.** Private banks state minimums (Coutts £3m for new clients, Barclays £3–5m, per Spear's, Apr 2026) and treat exceptions case by case [10]. A published "from" price and a stated scope both act as qualification.

## White-glove inclusions

**Signals (include, name, and keep inside the price)**
- A named senior expert, not a team (Ohio State's executive practice leads with small physician team and direct access [15]; Toza's single named expert is the stronger version).
- Home visit, in person, client present (concierge providers visit homes to set systems up [16]).
- Direct line, including evenings, weekends and holidays [15]; "guaranteed live person" in the integrator plan [13].
- Fast defined response: target scheduling within 48 hours on business days [15].
- Annual or semi-annual check-up [13]; extended annual review (60–90 minutes in concierge medicine [1]).
- Coordination with the client's other advisers (lawyers, wealth managers): custom, "coordination" tiers in the legal ladder [3]; dedicated coordinators in executive medicine [1].
- Private setting and discretion (private lobby, valet in the medical example [15]); for Toza: discreet invoicing, no CRM-style mailing, no names on documents (my calc).
- Travel and hardware stated as included, within defined scope.
- One accountable provider on one predictable contract; family offices say they want exactly this [5].

**Cheapeners (avoid)**
- Itemised extras and add-on fees: the medical sources warn that unpriced extras (labs, specialists) are the main surprise and complaint [1].
- Discount codes and promotions: tie to loss of trust and "betrayal" when a price later falls [8].
- Different prices by channel or by who asks [8].
- Free trials or free audits as the default entry: the high-end sources use paid diagnostics or interviews; some concierge practices do offer 30–90 day trials [1], so this is a norm to avoid, not a rule.
- Hourly meters visible to the client; hourly billing makes the expert look interchangeable [5][12] (my reading).
- Non-refundable language without explanation (reported in concierge medicine [1]); explain it as reserving capacity.

## Household and family patterns

- **People, not households, are the usual unit, with a falling per-person price.** DeleteMe: 1 person $129, 2 people $229, family up to 4 $329 per year (the second person adds about $100, three more add about $100 more; my calc) [17].
- **Two-person plan is a named step**, not an add-on: "solo / two people / family up to four", with larger groups going to custom pricing [17]. Toza's "Inner Circle" is the same logic at 4.
- **Concierge medicine splits.** Some practices discount spouses and children, others charge full price per member; example family of four at a mid-range practice is $8,000–20,000 per year [1]. Full-price-per-member is accepted at premium level.
- **Executive medicine prices per person** ($5,300–7,600 per person) [2].
- **Complexity, not headcount, is the cap in fixed-fee law.** Tiers move on assets and entities; blended families and international elements push at least to a higher tier [3]. For Toza, a 2-person tier should state included devices and accounts per person, and a "complex household" clause (staff, multiple residences, many devices) should lift the tier. A private-bank example describes a family with 35 devices as a case for a concierge [16].
- No source found on pricing staff or assistants as add-ons; treat as unverified design choice.

## Retainer and annual-care patterns

- **Retainer logic.** Retained contracts convert variable costs into one predictable line; providers selling only day rates keep chasing bookings [5].
- **Maintenance as a percentage of the original.** The only verified percentage I found is software: typically 20% or more of the discounted licence fee in the first year (examples 22% and 18%) [18]. That is an analogy, not a security-services benchmark.
- **Retainer client life.** Reported agency churn 18% (retainer) vs 42% (project), and lifespan 56 vs 24 months [12] (aggregator, originals unchecked). Directional only.
- **Take rate caution.** One integrator reported about 30 sign-ups in two months against 600+ managed systems, which is about 5% (my calc, and unclear base) [13]. Plan for a minority of past clients to join.
- **Inclusions typical of a post-project plan:** annual or semi-annual check-up, priority response, remote fixes, clear terms after the warranty ends [13].
- **Premium memberships are mostly paid upfront annually** [1].
- Typical price as a percentage of a security-hardening engagement: **not found; unverified.**

## Implications for Toza (numbered)

1. **Raise and round.** Evidence supports higher prices as a quality signal when tied to a story (one named expert, in person, scarce capacity) [7][8]. Use round, tidy figures; no ".90" endings [9]. P4 already shows the Shield path is under-priced.
2. **Sell capacity, say it plainly.** "Takes on only a few engagements a month" is the same scarcity mechanism as small concierge panels [1]; state it if true.
3. **Keep the Assessment paid and distinct**, positioned as the executive-physical analogue ($2,700–4,000 annually [2]) rather than a lead magnet. Consider making its credit partial (P4 option B), since high-end sources use paid diagnostics [2][3].
4. **Tier by household complexity, not just headcount.** Copy the legal ladder: stated included people, devices, accounts, and a top tier "from" with scoping [3]. Keep Inner Circle as "from".
5. **Put the 2-person tier in as a named step**, priced above 1.5x Shield, closer to full price per person at this level, since premium medicine accepts per-person pricing [1][2]. Falling per-person discounts are a mass-market pattern [17]. This is my judgement from the sources; test it.
6. **Define an urgent-response premium** (for example 25–50% for under 30–60 days' notice, borrowed from [3]) for dispute-driven buyers, rather than discounting.
7. **No coupons, no discount codes, no channel prices.** One public price per tier [8]. Give value (a scoped inclusion, an extra review) rather than a price cut when negotiating.
8. **Include, not itemise.** Travel in Israel, hardware at stated quantities (keys, router), discreet invoicing and a written scope stay inside the price. Only genuinely out-of-scope work (extra devices beyond the stated count) is a pre-priced extra, stated up front [1][3].
9. **Offer an annual Care plan only to past clients** (periodic re-check, priority response, refresh of keys or settings, after-hours line). Software maintenance norms (about 18–22% of the original [18]) give a floor for the analogy; for a hands-on re-check by the expert, a higher share is plausible, but this is untested. Illustrative range for planning, not evidence: about 20–35% of the original fee (my calc; Shield ₪14,000 gives about ₪2,800–4,900). Assume a minority of clients will take it [13]. Paid upfront annually [1].
10. **Do not sell it by the hour.** Retainers are rare among solo experts (about 13% use monthly [12]), so a simple annual plan is distinctive; bill as a flat annual amount with defined inclusions.
11. **Use thresholds as a filter**: a published "from" price and an intake conversation (like private banks' interview [10]) screens out price-shoppers, which suits 1–2 engagements a month.
12. **Fill the Israel gap.** No published ILS norms found. Ask 5–10 target buyers or their advisers (lawyers, family-office staff, private bankers) what they pay for private security, concierge or IT, and anchor Toza against "a few days of executive protection" or "an annual executive physical" rather than against IT rates [4][2].

## Limitations

Most sources are US/UK, secondary and marketing-adjacent; several estimates carry inconsistent figures (the concierge guide itself says to verify) [1]. Hebrew-language searches returned no usable prices. One article on UHNW retainers was paywalled; only its introduction was read [5]. No source gave a verified percentage for annual care of a security hardening engagement.
