# P1 — Pricing benchmarks for Toza tiering

Date: 2026-10-15. Basis: Toza fact base (`reference/llms.baseline.txt`) taken as fact. External figures are as published by third parties; USD unless stated. "Unverified" = secondary or single source. Nothing here is an invented price. Where I compute something, it is marked "(my calc)".

## Summary: findings relevant to Toza's tiering

1. **No close comparator exists.** I found no provider that sells a one-off, in-person, fixed-price personal hardening engagement. Premium personal cyber is sold as an annual service with monitoring. Toza's one-off model is a real differentiator, and it makes subscription anchors only partly comparable.
2. **The nearest premium anchors are annual and remote.** BlackCloak is reported at about $10,000 a year for remote management of home digital security (Boston Globe, 2025). Aprivé, a rival, is $5,000 a year with a network review, monitoring and a concierge. Toza's ₪14,000 (about $3,800–4,200; my calc, depends on FX) one-off sits below the annual cost of either.
3. **BlackCloak does not publish prices.** Its family-office page has no tiers and a contact/demo route only. A third-party aggregator says "$3,000 per user annually" (unverified, labelled by that page as third-party). Top-end personal cyber is "inquire" by default.
4. **Data-removal tools are the low anchor, and they publish prices.** DeleteMe: $129/yr for 1 person, $329/yr for a family of up to 4. Incogni: $95.88–$275.88/yr. Tiers there differ by people covered and by custom-removal scope, not by outcome. The jump from about $130–330 a year to Toza's ₪3,600 is a change of category, which should be made explicit in copy.
5. **Tiers in premium services differ by scope of attention, not by features.** Concierge medicine: about $600–2,000 (hybrid), $2,000–6,000 (mid), $6,000–25,000+ (executive), with panels sometimes under 100 patients (secondary source, estimates). Executive tiers sell smaller client panels and dedicated coordination.
6. **A diagnostic that is separate from membership is standard in the executive space.** PartnerMD sells an annual executive physical (half or full day) and a continuous membership as two distinct things, and bundles them. This supports the owner's view: the assessment is a first step, not a tier.
7. **Fixed-fee professional packages are named by what the client gets, in plain language.** LeanLaw's divorce example: Forms Only, Attorney Review, Full Representation. It publishes ranges and recommends public prices for trust. Tiers are defined by inclusions, exclusions, and who each suits.
8. **Top tiers are normally "inquire" or "from".** BlackCloak and PartnerMD show no price. Toza already uses "from ₪42,000" and "individually scoped", which matches the norm.
9. **Israeli senior-expert hourly rates are low relative to Toza's implied rates.** A 2026 freelancer guide gives ₪320–520 per hour and ₪2,800–4,500 per day for cyber specialists, with senior AI/security at ₪4,800+ per day (secondary, "market ranges"). Toza's rate is a premium on a one-expert, in-person, discreet basis, so its price is not a time-and-materials price. Copy should sell the outcome and the adviser, not hours.
10. **Diagnostics are sometimes bundled or free.** JPMorgan Private Bank gives cyber help to ultra-wealthy clients as a relationship benefit ([4]); a free "Executive Digital Protection Assessment" is listed on Gumroad (content unverified, [17]). Toza's paid, credited assessment contrasts with free lead-gen.

## Tiering patterns, with examples

**A. Diagnostic first, then ongoing relationship (executive health).** PartnerMD pairs a half-day "Enhanced" or full-day "Signature" physical with a membership, priced by quote only ([8]). The physical is described as periodic and one-time; the membership as continuous. Lesson: the diagnostic has its own name and format, and the relationship product is separate.

**B. Fee ladder by attention and panel size (concierge medicine).** Three bands, roughly $600–2,000, $2,000–6,000, $6,000–25,000+ per year; the premium band adds advanced diagnostics and dedicated coordination ([9], secondary estimates). Family pricing varies: some discount, some charge per member. The top band is a "+" range, not a fixed price.

**C. Plain-language fixed-fee ladder (boutique legal).** Forms Only, Attorney Review, Full Representation, with typical ranges of $500–1,200, $1,500–3,000, $3,500–7,500 ([10]). Each tier lists inclusions, exclusions, and who it suits; complex cases get sub-tiers by number of children, assets or business valuation. A UK family firm (Barcan+Kirby) advertises "standard or tailored" fixed-fee packages with no figures on the page ([11]). So both published ranges and "call us" exist side by side.

**D. Per-head scaling.** Data-removal tools price by the number of people (1, 2, up to 4 or 5), with family plans costing much less than the sum of individuals (DeleteMe: $329 for four versus $129 for one) ([5][6]). Concierge medicine family pricing is inconsistent ([9]). Toza's Inner Circle (4 people, 8 devices, 8 keys, network) is already a per-head bundle; its ₪42,000 is three times Personal Shield's ₪14,000 for four times the people, plus network.

**E. Anchoring and decoy effects.** Simon-Kucher describes anchoring (the first price seen sets the reference) and decoy pricing (a near-premium option makes the premium look better value) ([12]). Conditions it names: the anchor is met before the comparison, tiers have clear differences, and use is transparent. That article is about commercial pricing generally, not luxury services; treat it as a conditional heuristic. For an adviser-register brand, a visible top tier works as an anchor; an overt "decoy" tier would clash with the quiet register and the transparency Toza's own copy promises.

**F. Credit of the diagnostic.** No premium-services benchmark found for crediting a diagnostic fee against the engagement.

## Israeli context

- **Rates.** Cyber specialists as freelancers: ₪320–520 per hour; ₪2,800–4,500 per day; senior AI/security from ₪4,800 per day; foreign-client billing typically 20–40% higher; VAT treatment not stated ([13], secondary, "not fixed tariffs"). Public-sector tenders require hourly shekel pricing excluding VAT (Maccabi cyber-strategy consulting tender, [14]), but the actual bid figures were blank. Recruiter salary data ([15]) is employee pay, not a consulting rate.
- **Toza implied rates (my calc, VAT 18% as stated on [13]):** Assessment ₪3,600 incl. VAT is about ₪3,050 net, roughly ₪760 per hour over about four hours. Personal Shield ₪14,000 incl. VAT is about ₪11,860 net, roughly ₪850–1,700 per hour over 7–14 hours. These are about 2–5 times the freelancer hourly band, which fits a private-adviser premium and a bundled product (keys, record, support) rather than billable time.
- **Israeli premium B2C price display.** Not established. The one example (private airport terminal, 2019, USD, lower rate per additional person, [16]) is old and not cyber. Open gap.

## Implications for Toza

1. **Name the assessment as a step, not a tier.** Use "First step" or "Private Exposure Assessment" on its own row, visually outside the tier set. Keep the sentence "credited within 14 days". Model: PartnerMD's physical versus membership split ([8]).
2. **Show three protection tiers: Personal Shield, Inner Circle Shield, Bespoke Private Protection.** Keep the existing names; they read as private-bank products, not software plans. Avoid "Basic/Pro/Enterprise".
3. **Differentiate tiers by who is protected and how much of the household is in scope, not by feature counts.** People, devices, keys, and home network are Toza's real differences. Add the adviser's time ("one adviser, in person") as a constant across tiers, since concierge tiers differ by attention, not by a different product.
4. **Show prices for the two fixed tiers and "from" for Inner Circle.** This follows the legal-package lesson (published prices build trust, [10]) and Toza's own transparent register. Show Bespoke as "individually scoped" with a one-line statement of what triggers it (more than four people, multiple properties, family office, business entity). Do not invent a "from" figure for Bespoke without owner decision; see open questions.
5. **Keep the top tier as scoped, but let Inner Circle's "from ₪42,000" act as the visible anchor.** It is the highest published number, seen after the Assessment and Shield. This uses anchoring without a decoy ([12]).
6. **Do not add a recurring plan to the main ladder.** Competitors' annual fees (BlackCloak about $10,000, Aprivé $5,000) are what Toza is positioned against; being one-off is the contrast. Keep "optional follow-up" as a separate, individually scoped line after a completed service, as the baseline already says. A subscription would also conflict with "does not monitor your devices".
7. **Make the low-anchor contrast explicit.** Data-removal tools cost about $100–330 a year; Toza's copy should say what they do not do (in-person setup, keys, account access changes, teaching) without naming competitors or disparaging them.
8. **Express value in outcome language, not hours.** Israeli freelance hourly rates are ₪320–520, so any hours-based framing invites comparison with a far lower number. The baseline's "7–14 work hours" should stay as a scope statement, not a price justification.
9. **Per-head messaging for Inner Circle.** ₪42,000 for four people is ₪10,500 per person, below Personal Shield's ₪14,000 per person (my calc). Say "for four people and the home network" and let the reader see the family economy; do not show a per-person price.

## Open questions

1. Is the owner willing to show any figure for Bespoke ("from ₪...")? No benchmark settles this; concierge and executive products mostly show none, and legal packages show ranges.
2. Does the owner want any published statement of annual-equivalent value (e.g., "one-off, no annual fee")? It is accurate but invites a subscription comparison.
3. Which Israeli providers of personal cyber for individuals exist? None found; a Hebrew-language directory or Facebook/LinkedIn scan, or direct enquiry, would be needed.
4. Fortalice, USI and 360 Privacy pricing: not located in citable form. Mystery-shopper enquiry is the route.
5. Do affluent Israeli buyers expect public prices? Not established. The owner's own client feedback is the best evidence.
6. Is the 14-day credit window right? No external benchmark found.
