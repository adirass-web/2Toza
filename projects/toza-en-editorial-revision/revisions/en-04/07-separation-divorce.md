# Trust can end before access does.

Your plans for a separate life may still sit in accounts and services set up for a shared one. Private conversations and documents can become a source of pressure if that access remains open.

Dr. Lior Tabansky works with you, in person, to examine relevant access and carry out agreed changes. You do not have to untangle the technical details before asking for help.

[Request a private conversation](#contact)

In person · With you present · You control your credentials

## Choose which access you want to keep

A shared family service may remain useful after separation. It may also connect accounts or devices in ways that leave messages, photos, documents or location information accessible.

Dr. Tabansky examines relevant devices, accounts and recovery arrangements within the agreed scope. He explains what they allow and which changes affect one another, so you can distinguish access you still need from access you want to end.

Such access can remain without a new intrusion. Its presence does not establish that your spouse has used it or explain how someone learned a particular fact.

## Make changes with their consequences in view

A password change may leave another route open. Changing a family service can affect access you still need, and some changes generate notifications or become visible to other users.

We explain what the relevant access allows and agree the sequence with you. You can decide what to change with a clearer understanding of the effects. We do not promise invisible changes.
