## Required findings from the first pass — resolved

### 1. Moderate — Home / Business teaser: authorization is not the entire service boundary

**Text:** “Examine the personal and work access you are authorized to manage.”

**Issue:** The accepted teaser concerns personal accounts connected to business circumstances. This candidate can read as general examination of all personal and corporate access the reader can authorize. A business owner may authorize a very large company environment; permission alone does not bring it into the personal service or the two-device scope. The linked Business page correctly excludes a corporate security audit and counter-espionage engagement, but the teaser should make the same category promise.

**Required fix:** Keep the concrete commercial-leverage stake. Anchor the intervention to the person's own identities/devices/accounts and their connections to business access, within agreed scope. Do not introduce company-system coverage merely by saying it is authorized.

**Sweep:** clarity, relevance, authority and expectation management. **Owner decision needed:** no; this restores current authority without weakening the selected pain.

**Resolution verified:** The final candidate says, “Examine how your personal devices and digital identities connect to work, within your authority and agreed scope.” This restores the personal/business intersection and preserves the commercial stake. RESOLVED.

### 2. Moderate — Home / old-copy distinction: qualify which copies persist independently

**Text:** “Changing an account does not remove a copy of a private document already stored elsewhere, such as on an older computer or backup.”

**Issue:** The intended distinction is useful and materially missing from the baseline. However, “a copy ... stored elsewhere” can also describe a synchronized copy whose state may change with its source. The absolute construction describes all such copies as independent. It should communicate why changing current access cannot be assumed to retrieve information already retained elsewhere, without suggesting every stored copy behaves identically.

**Required fix:** Specify independently retained/downloaded/backup copies, or use the bounded point that changing current account access does not necessarily remove copies already retained elsewhere. Keep the claim about persistence, not a new promise to search additional devices, inspect archives or delete other people's holdings.

**Sweep:** specificity, technical credibility and claim precision. **Owner decision needed:** no; this is a narrower expression of the owner's explanation.

**Resolution verified:** The final candidate says, “An independent copy of a private document can remain on an old computer or in a backup after you change the account it came from.” “Independent” and “can remain” remove the overgeneralization without promising additional device work, archive examination or deletion. RESOLVED.

There are **no major/blocker findings** and no unresolved required correction in the final public-copy candidate. More cases or interviews are not required to present it for owner review.

## Recommended improvements, not blockers

### 3. Moderate editorial opportunity — Home / “They may not need to hack you.”

This retained heading is defensible: “may” covers ordinary permission and retained copies without claiming all exposure is non-hacking. It is not a factual failure by itself.

Its new paragraph, however, now discusses personal knowledge used to obtain account access and gain trust. Some readers reasonably classify social engineering and account compromise as hacking. The heading therefore asks the unaware reader to resolve an unnecessary technical distinction.

**Recommendation:** Use a heading about how familiar people or ordinary arrangements can create access. Preserve the concrete mechanisms and avoid teaching a rule that social engineering is categorically “not hacking.” This can be changed without another owner/factual decision if the drafting authorization includes editorial improvements.

**Resolution verified:** The final heading is “Everyday connections can reveal more than you expect.” The adjacent text avoids repeating it verbatim. The unnecessary technical distinction is removed. RESOLVED.

### 4. Minor — Home metadata / purchase distinction

“In-person assessment, hardening and practical teaching” is an accurate list of Toza's services, but a search snippet does less than the baseline to distinguish initial assessment from separately purchased full hardening. The page itself makes the distinction explicitly.

**Recommendation:** If space permits, retain a compact separate-stage signal in the description. Not a claim conflict: it does not attach all listed work to the Assessment price.

**Resolution verified:** The final Description reads, “In-person assessment with Dr. Lior Tabansky, approved urgent fixes within scope, and separately agreed hardening and teaching.” The compact purchase distinction and urgent-work qualification are explicit. RESOLVED.

### 5. Minor packet defect — Home density note

The note still said approximately 900 words / about 4% expansion in the last full file read. Final FACT-CHECK.json, after the metadata fix, records 930 versus 865: approximately **7.5%** longer. Pricing is 1724 versus 1706: approximately **1.1%** longer.

**Recommendation:** Correct the annotation before presenting the packet. Do not claim the page is shorter. The additional meaning earns some length, although another rendering pass must assess its actual reading effort.

No material AI/cyber cliché defect warrants a gate failure. “The situation changes the work” is broad when scanned alone, but its following paragraph makes it specific. The new paired action headline is a deliberate compact construction, not sufficient evidence of synthetic prose.

**Recommended cuts:** none required for this gate.
