# Toza Editorial Harness Calibration — EN-02-v1

Calibration corpus: `TOZA_EN_COPY_REVIEW.md`, review set EN-02-v1, dated 4 October 2026.  
Benchmark: `toza-upgrade-english-copy-spec-revised-v2.md`.  
Protected reference: Initial Paid Visit / PA-01-v2, marked already approved in the corpus.

## Executive verdict

The corpus is substantially aligned with the strategic direction, but it cannot be treated as a simple implementation of the August spec.

The calibration exposed three categories of issue:

1. **Authority drift:** newer approved commercial facts now conflict with the older spec.
2. **Cross-page over-repetition:** consistency has been achieved partly by copying operational paragraphs onto many pages.
3. **Premium dilution by explanation density:** the service is credible and careful, but repeated qualification/boundary language sometimes overwhelms the rare-senior-service signal.

The harness must therefore distinguish:
- stale spec vs stale copy;
- necessary standardized operational wording vs redundant persuasion;
- protected approved copy vs unapproved draft;
- factual consistency edits vs stylistic edits.

Those distinctions have been added to the doctrine/editor/red-team skills.

## 3. Cross-page repetition finding

Approximate corpus counts from EN-02-v1:
- “Request a private conversation”: 29 occurrences.
- “₪3,600”: 37 occurrences.
- “Personal Shield”: 42 occurrences.
- “Inner Circle Shield”: 21 occurrences.
- first-conversation/no-technical-work formulation: about 11 occurrences.
- cloud-analysis/log-retention disclaimer: about 11 occurrences.
- authorized-digital-environment language: about 11 occurrences.

Interpretation:
- prices and shared legal/operational conditions may need repetition;
- the persuasive narrative should not feel templated;
- several situation pages currently repeat full contact, boundary and service mechanics that could be shortened or linked once the information architecture is stable.

Harness change:
- added a corpus repetition policy and Compression/Density sweep.

## 4. Premium signal

Strengths:
- direct named expert;
- in-person delivery;
- client presence and approval;
- explicit credential control;
- precise written scope before payment;
- separate purchase decisions;
- verification and records;
- clear limits.

Weaknesses:
- the corpus repeatedly explains process and limitations at a level that can make the service feel administratively careful rather than rare;
- founder authority is strongest on Why Toza and weaker on some situation pages;
- limited capacity/scarcity is not currently used as a public proof signal;
- several pages read as highly responsible service documentation rather than high-end editorial sales copy.

Harness change:
- added Prestige/Scarcity sweep;
- red-team asks whether copy feels like rare senior service or merely competent cybersecurity consultancy.

## 5. Emotional effect

Strengths:
- concrete examples: old device, recovery email, shared app, location, folders;
- non-melodramatic;
- avoids accusation;
- good recognition language on separation/business/they-know-something pages.

Weakness:
- relief/control is often implicit rather than felt;
- operational explanation can crowd out the emotional transition from uncertainty to control.

Harness change:
- editor now explicitly checks recognition → clarity → relief/control.

## 6. AI / formulaic-language risk

No major generic AI clichés dominate the corpus.

However, repetition creates an AI-like effect even when individual sentences are good:
- same CTA block;
- same “first conversation checks fit…” formulation;
- same privacy paragraph;
- same “does not investigate / reconstruct / collect evidence / give legal advice” sequence.

Harness change:
- Human Credibility sweep evaluates corpus-level templating, not only sentence-level clichés.
