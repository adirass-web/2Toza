# Toza Editorial Doctrine

Status: draft governing doctrine for the editorial harness

## 1. Category and audience

Toza is a premium, private, in-person personal digital defense/protection service for individuals entering or already inside consequential personal or business disputes.

Primary commercial audience:
- affluent and high-net-worth private clients;
- founders, executives, professionals and families;
- people who value discretion, direct senior expertise and personal service;
- people who understand that their situation matters but cannot reliably see the full digital exposure around them.

Internal audience model: **situation-aware, exposure-blind**.

Do not position Toza as broad consumer cybersecurity, enterprise cybersecurity, routine IT support, SaaS, forensic investigation, counter-surveillance, evidence collection, legal advice, or a domestic-abuse/safety service.

## 2. Core proposition

**Ordinary access becomes leverage.**

The reader usually understands the dispute. The blind spot is the network of trusted devices, live sessions, recovery routes, shared services, permissions, connected identities and personal/work crossover that has accumulated through normal use.

Toza’s value is expert interpretation and intervention:
- identify consequential access;
- explain what matters and why;
- sequence changes deliberately;
- address urgent exposure within scope;
- harden what requires broader intervention;
- verify and explain the resulting setup.

## 3. Core mechanism

**They may not need to hack you.**

Residual access may be entirely ordinary in origin. Marketing copy must not turn exposure into accusation. Never infer that a spouse, partner, relative, investigator, colleague or commercial counterparty has acted improperly merely because an access path exists.

## 4. Service logic

**Map. Close and harden. Verify.**

The Private Exposure Assessment begins the method. Personal Shield or another separately accepted full service completes broader hardening when justified.

Qualification, paid assessment and broader protection are commercially distinct.

## 5. Premium signal

Premium must be demonstrated through operating reality, not luxury adjectives.

Preferred proof:
- one named senior expert;
- direct work with Dr. Lior Tabansky;
- in person, with the client present;
- no junior handoffs in technical delivery;
- tailored mapping before intervention;
- explained and approved material changes;
- client control of credentials;
- defined scope and completion conditions;
- supplied/configured security keys where included;
- verification and a dated record where included;
- client-authorized attorney/adviser coordination;
- limited engagement capacity where factually supportable;
- travel by arrangement where applicable.

Avoid: luxury, elite, white-glove, world-class, best-in-class, bespoke as an empty adjective, military-grade, cutting-edge.

## 6. Desired reader progression

**Recognition → relevance → confidence → trust → relief/control → private contact.**

Not:
**fear → urgency → conversion.**

The copy may acknowledge pressure, uncertainty and fear of making the wrong change. It should move toward clarity, control, reduced uncertainty and confidence in the next step.

## 7. Voice

Copy should be:
- plain;
- calm;
- direct;
- adult;
- warm without reassurance clichés;
- technically credible without sounding academic;
- premium through specificity and restraint;
- non-accusatory;
- non-alarmist.

Write for an intelligent reader. Do not explain obvious life circumstances back to them. Do not infantilize. Do not manufacture empathy.

## 8. Evidence and claims

Every consequential claim must be one of:
- directly supported;
- clearly qualified;
- removed;
- escalated for owner/factual decision.

Privacy-compatible proof is valid proof. Testimonials and named case studies are not required.

Never imply:
- a confirmed breach where none is established;
- attribution;
- a guarantee that all exposure will be found or removed;
- certification where the deliverable is only a record;
- invisible changes;
- remote delivery where the service is in-person;
- a response-time SLA beyond an actually approved commitment.

## 9. Authority precedence

When editing against multiple documents:

1. explicit later owner approval;
2. newer approved commercial/service fact;
3. current written offer/product rule;
4. strategic/editorial specification;
5. existing unapproved draft copy;
6. external copywriting framework.

If a later approved artifact conflicts with an older spec, flag **authority conflict / spec stale**. Do not “correct” the newer approved copy back to the older fact.

Current governing commercial decisions, confirmed by the owner on 4 October 2026:
- Private Exposure Assessment: **₪3,600 including VAT**.
- Assessment scope: **one main phone + one other device: a PC, iPad or second phone**.
- Public duration language: **at least two hours in person; typically four**, while completion remains outcome-based around the agreed intake, mapping, diagnosis and approved urgent first aid.
- **Inner Circle Shield** is approved product architecture, starting at **₪42,000 including VAT**, with the current EN-02-v1 scope.
- The full eligible Assessment fee may be credited toward **Personal Shield, Inner Circle Shield or Bespoke Private Protection** when the current credit conditions are met.

These five decisions supersede conflicting language in the August strategic specification. Treat the older specification as stale on those points. Do not reopen them as authority conflicts unless a later owner decision changes them.

A consolidated replacement specification should be created later so the strategic spec and current commercial authority no longer diverge.

## 10. Repetition policy

Shared operational language may be standardized where consistency matters:
- contact qualification boundary;
- credential control;
- legal/service boundaries;
- privacy/data-handling statements;
- commercial prices and credit conditions.

Persuasive/explanatory copy must not repeat mechanically across pages.

Ask:
- Does this page need the full statement?
- Can the shared fact be linked rather than repeated?
- Is repetition required for informed decision-making at this point?
- Does this paragraph add page-specific meaning?

## 11. AI and cliché prohibitions

Flag:
- “In today’s digital world”;
- “navigate” as vague life/business copy;
- “empower”;
- “peace of mind”;
- “protect what matters most”;
- “stay one step ahead”;
- “comprehensive protection” without scope;
- “whether you are…”;
- “not just X, but Y” / “more than X, it is Y” patterns;
- artificial triples;
- excessive em-dash drama;
- fragments used only to imitate sophistication;
- repeated rhetorical questions;
- generic “designed to” filler;
- empty prestige claims.

## 12. Compression rule

Every page must earn its length.

Remove:
- duplicate argument;
- duplicate reassurance;
- repeated service mechanics better handled by a dedicated page;
- legal/operational detail that can be linked without harming informed consent;
- headings that merely restate the previous heading.

Compression must not remove material price, scope, boundary or purchase information required for a responsible decision.

## 13. Cross-page consistency

Audit globally for:
- service names;
- price and VAT treatment;
- device scope;
- duration language;
- urgent response trigger;
- credit condition;
- security-key counts;
- support period;
- deliverable names;
- attorney authorization;
- credential control;
- privacy wording;
- retired categories;
- CTA wording.

A corpus-level contradiction is higher priority than local elegance.

## 14. Protected approved copy

When a section/page is explicitly marked approved, editing defaults to:
- no stylistic rewrite;
- factual consistency check;
- spelling/grammar defect flag;
- conflict escalation.

Any proposed substantive change to approved copy must be separately identified, never silently incorporated.
