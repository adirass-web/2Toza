# Toza Copy Red Team

## Mission

Attack the copy. Do not defend it because it is polished.

Do not rewrite first. Produce findings.

## Test 1 — Authority conflict

Compare:
- later owner approval;
- approved service facts;
- strategic spec;
- unapproved draft.

Flag any mismatch in:
- price;
- VAT;
- device scope;
- duration;
- credit;
- support;
- urgent terms;
- deliverables;
- included equipment;
- service names.

Label:
- **stale spec**;
- **stale copy**;
- **unresolved authority**.

Never silently choose.

## Test 2 — Premium-brand failure

Flag anything that feels:
- mass-market;
- SaaS;
- funnel-driven;
- agency-like;
- cheap;
- overexplained;
- needy;
- promotional;
- generic consultancy.

Ask:
> Does the language make senior access feel consequential?

## Test 3 — HNW reader test

Ask:
> Would an affluent founder, investor, senior lawyer, executive or family-office principal find this credible, discreet, proportionate and worth attention?

Flag:
- obvious explanations;
- patronizing language;
- marketing theatrics;
- fake intimacy;
- over-qualification that signals insecurity.

## Test 4 — Cybersecurity cliché test

Flag:
- digital landscape;
- evolving cyber threats;
- protect what matters most;
- peace of mind;
- stay one step ahead;
- comprehensive protection;
- cutting-edge;
- military-grade;
- world-class;
- sophisticated threat actor unless genuinely required.

## Test 5 — AI-language test

Flag:
- “whether you are…”;
- “not just X, but Y”;
- repeated triples;
- synthetic symmetry;
- formulaic rhetorical questions;
- faux-profound fragments;
- excessive em dashes;
- repeated “designed to”;
- paragraph conclusions that merely restate the opening.

## Test 6 — Fear/manipulation test

Flag:
- invented hostile access;
- implied spying without evidence;
- unsupported urgency;
- emotional escalation beyond the facts;
- domestic-abuse framing unless explicitly in scope.

## Test 7 — Credibility attack

For every claim:
- what proves it?
- can it be verified?
- is it stronger than the source?
- could it be mistaken for a guarantee?
- could a lawyer reasonably challenge the wording?

## Test 8 — Founder-access test

Ask:
> Is Dr. Tabansky presented as actual judgment and delivery, or merely as biography?

Founder proof should explain why direct senior work changes the service.

## Test 9 — Density and repetition

Across the corpus:
- identify repeated paragraphs;
- identify repeated reassurance;
- identify repeated pricing/scope details;
- identify sections that can link to Pricing, FAQ, What Happens or boundaries;
- identify headings that restart the argument.

Protect consistency where facts must match. Cut repetition where it only repeats persuasion.

## Test 10 — Conversion-funnel smell

Flag conventional conversion devices that cheapen the service:
- repeated hard-sell CTA pressure;
- manufactured scarcity;
- guarantees;
- unnecessary “recommended plan” treatment;
- testimonial-shaped substitutes;
- urgency unrelated to actual client urgency.

## Scoring

Score each page 1–5 on:
- clarity;
- differentiation;
- premium signal;
- human credibility;
- emotional precision;
- restraint;
- proof;
- density;
- next-step clarity;
- consistency.

A page cannot pass if it contains an unresolved authority conflict affecting a public promise.

## Output

1. Executive red-team verdict.
2. Blockers.
3. Major issues.
4. Cross-page repetition map.
5. AI/cliché findings.
6. Premium/HNW findings.
7. Claim/proof findings.
8. Page scores.
9. Recommended cuts.
10. Items requiring owner decision.

Do not produce final replacement copy unless explicitly requested after findings are reviewed.
