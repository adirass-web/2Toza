## 1. Strategic doctrine

### Category

Toza is a **premium, private, in-person digital defense service** for individuals entering or already inside consequential personal or business disputes, including separation or divorce, inheritance conflict, and founder or partner disputes.

The primary commercial audience is affluent and high-net-worth private clients who value discretion, senior expertise and direct personal service. Keep this as an internal targeting rule unless a specific page benefits from saying it explicitly.

The public category can use **personal digital protection** where a less technical phrase improves comprehension. Do not position Toza as broad consumer cybersecurity, enterprise cybersecurity, IT support, a SaaS product, a forensic investigation service, a counter-surveillance service, or a domestic-abuse/safety service.

### Core proposition

**Ordinary access becomes leverage.**

The client usually understands why the situation has become sensitive. What the client cannot reasonably see is the network of trusted devices, live sessions, recovery paths, shared services, permissions, connected identities and personal/work crossover that has accumulated around them.

Internal audience model:

**Situation-aware. Exposure-blind.**

The client does not need to be educated from zero about the dispute. The value of Toza is expert interpretation of the client’s own digital environment: finding consequential exposure the client cannot reliably self-diagnose, sequencing changes safely, and hardening what matters.

### Core mechanism

**They may not need to hack you.**

Serious personal digital exposure can arise from access that was once ordinary: a trusted device, a signed-in session, a recovery method, a family or cloud service, a shared account, an old permission, or an identity that spans personal and business systems.

Do not imply that hostile access has occurred unless independently established outside public marketing copy. The marketing frame is exposure and residual access, not accusation.

### Primary service logic

**Map. Close and harden. Verify.**

- **Map:** understand the client’s situation, inventory the relevant digital environment, identify consequential exposure, and perform approved urgent first aid where necessary.
- **Close and harden:** remove or replace inappropriate access, harden the in-scope devices, identities and apps, migrate critical identity/recovery paths where needed, and configure physical security keys.
- **Verify:** test the changes, complete the applicable hardening checklist, train the client, and record the result.

The **Private Exposure Assessment** begins the method.  
**Personal Shield** completes the methodical hardening when broader intervention is justified.

### Premium signal

Premium positioning must come from the operating model, not luxury adjectives:

- One named senior expert, start to finish.
- No junior technicians and no handoffs.
- In person, with the client present.
- Tailored exposure mapping before intervention.
- Material changes explained before execution.
- Client remains in control of credentials.
- Two physical security keys supplied, configured and tested in Personal Shield.
- Formal client record on completion of Personal Shield.
- Attorney or adviser coordination only with client authorization.
- Limited engagement capacity.
- Client travel by arrangement.

Do not publish a numeric “1–2 clients per month” capacity claim. Public language may state that Toza accepts **a limited number of private engagements each month**.

- Use **Private Exposure Assessment** for the ₪3,500 entry service.
- Use **first aid** only for urgent exposure addressed during the assessment.
- Use **Personal Shield** for methodical hardening.
- Use **private dated record** only for a completed Personal Shield engagement.
- Use **brief plain-English summary** for the assessment outcome.
- Use **attorney-friendly summary** only when authorized by the client.
- Use **ordinary access becomes leverage** as a core positioning line.
- Use **They may not need to hack you** as the anticipatory category wedge.
- Use **Nobody had to hack anything** only in retrospective or recognition copy where the context does not imply a confirmed hostile event.
- Use **situation-aware, exposure-blind** only as internal strategy language unless specifically approved for public copy.
- Avoid `reset` as the governing service term.
- Avoid `Personal Cyber Shield`; the product name is **Personal Shield**.
- Avoid `Certificate of Hardening` unless a separate legal/product decision explicitly approves that name and defines it as a record of work performed rather than a security certification.
- Avoid confirmed breach, threat actor, investigation, forensic conclusion, counter-espionage, guaranteed security, or claims that hostile access has been proved.
- Avoid language that assumes wrongdoing by a spouse, partner, relative, investigator or commercial counterparty.
- Avoid “luxury,” “elite,” “white glove,” or similar generic premium adjectives unless a specific page needs them and the claim is substantiated by the service design.

### Vocabulary discipline

- **Exposure** is the umbrella condition. When the mechanism is known, name the mechanism instead of repeating `exposure`: access, signed-in session, recovery path, shared service, permission, trusted device, identity link, account control, information visibility, or personal/work crossover.
- Reserve **digital position** mainly for business-dispute, attorney-facing, or other professional copy. On general private-client pages prefer concrete language such as what remains open, what still connects, what can still be reached, and what should change first.
- **Expert exposure mapping** is a useful internal/service concept, not mandatory public wording. Public copy should usually describe what is mapped in concrete terms.
- Public-facing pages should prefer ordinary mechanisms. Terms such as `threat model`, `IoC`, `asset inventory`, `remediation`, `benchmark`, and `STIG` belong primarily in technical, professional, or internal material.

## 7. First contact, qualification, purchase and booking

The CTA **Request a private conversation** is a qualification step, not a purchase.

### Step 1: Initial message

The client sends a short, neutral message through WhatsApp or Signal. The first message should not contain passwords, recovery codes, legal documents, intimate material, screenshots or detailed allegations.

### Step 2: Personal reply

Dr. Lior Tabansky replies personally as soon as possible.

Public copy should not promise a fixed response-time SLA for routine inquiries unless one is operationally guaranteed. The urgent four-hour service is a separate availability promise: it means **in-person arrival within four hours after Toza accepts the urgent engagement and payment is received**, not four hours from the client's first message.

### Step 3: Brief private qualification conversation

The purpose of the initial conversation is to determine:

- Whether the situation is within Toza’s scope.
- Whether there appears to be an urgent issue requiring accelerated handling.
- Whether the Private Exposure Assessment is the appropriate next step.
- Basic geography, timing and device/logistics fit.
- Any immediate reason the engagement should not proceed.

This conversation is **not the Private Exposure Assessment**. It does not include device inspection, detailed exposure mapping, diagnosis, technical remediation or a substantive review of the client’s digital environment.

Do not give away the paid Assessment through an extended free consultation.

### Step 4: Clear offer before purchase

If the situation is a fit, the client receives a concise written engagement summary before paying. It should state:

- The recommended service.
- Price.
- Scope.
- What is included.
- What is not included.
- Any urgent, weekend, holiday or travel surcharge that applies.
- Payment terms.
- The next scheduling step.

The client is not committed merely because they contacted Toza or completed the qualification conversation.

### Step 5: Explicit purchase

The Assessment is purchased only when the client explicitly accepts the engagement and pays the ₪3,500 fee.

No device review, diagnosis or Assessment work begins before that point, except for narrowly defined urgent handling if Toza has separately accepted and documented an emergency engagement.

### Step 6: Booking and preparation

After payment:

- The appointment is scheduled.
- The client receives concise preparation instructions.
- Any case-specific pre-visit guidance is provided directly by Dr. Tabansky.
- The client is told what devices, access, chargers, security information and other practical items to have available, without sending sensitive credentials in advance.

### Step 7: Assessment outcome

At the end of the Assessment, the client receives the brief plain-English summary and understands:

- What was reviewed.
- What urgent changes were made.
- What remains.
- Whether broader methodical hardening is justified.

### Step 8: Separate Personal Shield decision

Personal Shield is a second, explicit purchase decision.

If broader hardening is recommended, Toza explains the work and confirms the ₪14,000 package scope. The client may accept immediately or decide later. There is no automatic continuation from Assessment to Personal Shield.

The ₪3,500 Assessment fee is credited in full toward Personal Shield when the full package begins within 14 days.

This sequence preserves the distinction:

**Conversation = qualification.**  
**Assessment = paid diagnosis, mapping and urgent first aid.**  
**Personal Shield = paid methodical hardening.**

## 12. Editorial rules

### Tone

Copy should be:

- Plain.
- Calm.
- Direct.
- Adult.
- Warm without reassurance clichés.
- Technically credible without sounding academic.
- Premium through specificity and restraint.
- Non-accusatory.
- Non-alarmist.

### Emotional range

The copy may use stronger emotion than neutral professional-services language when it reflects the client’s real situation. Appropriate emotional territory includes uncertainty, pressure, loss of control, fear of making the wrong change, frustration after partial self-help, and the relief that comes from understanding what remains open and regaining control.

Emotion should come from recognizable situations and consequences, not invented surveillance scenarios, melodrama, or unsupported claims that another person has acted improperly.

Preferred emotional outcomes are **relief, control, clarity, confidence in the next step, and reduced uncertainty**. Avoid generic promises of “peace of mind.”

### Avoid

- Explaining to sophisticated clients that they are ignorant, lazy or careless.
- Generic fear language.
- Cybersecurity jargon where ordinary language is stronger.
- Long lists of technical standards in conversion copy.
- Claims that imply certification, warranty or guaranteed security.
- Claims that imply Toza has proved who accessed what.
- Overuse of “digital life.”
- Overuse of “exposure” within the same paragraph.
- Repeating the same three-step method on every page without adaptation.
- Abstract nouns where a concrete example is clearer.
- Luxury vocabulary used as a substitute for proof.

### Prefer

- Concrete access mechanisms.
- Specific consequences.
- Short declarative headings.
- One idea per paragraph.
- Explicit distinction between what the client knows about the situation and what Toza discovers about the exposure.
- “Ordinary access becomes leverage” where the page needs the central proposition.
- “They may not need to hack you” where the page needs the category wedge.
- “One senior expert, start to finish” as proof of service design.
