# 1a Benchmarks: reference sites for the "quiet private adviser" register

Method: homepages fetched 2026-10-15 via WebFetch, which returns text and structure, not rendered pixels. Typography, colour and mobile layout are therefore mostly **unverified**; where stated, they come from page markup or copy cues. Mobile first-screen could not be checked for any site. Paraphrased throughout. Blocked: hoaregovett.com (robots), and that domain was in any case a lab-fit-out firm, not the bank (excluded).

## 1. Cross-cutting patterns (fit the register)

1. **Heritage and independence stand in for proof.** Founding year, family ownership, "independent" status. Coutts, Pictet, C. Hoare, Lombard Odier. Toza equivalent: one named expert, one dated record, nothing borrowed.
2. **No hard-sell CTA; soft text links.** Pictet ("find out more"), Lombard Odier ("discover"), Gavin de Becker (no button at all, only "Contact Us" in the nav). Contrast: BlackCloak, Fortalice and USI each use a repeated demo or "speak with an expert" button.
3. **Prices absent across all 10 homepages.** Banks and law firms hide them (Withers has a footer link to price and service information). Even USI's tier cards say "Inquire for Pricing". Toza showing ₪ amounts calmly would be a genuine differentiator, not a norm to copy.
4. **Short, declarative, low-key headlines.** Coutts uses clipped fragments; Withers a single "Welcome" and short sentences; Pictet a reflective question about calm. No fear statistics (Pictet, Coutts, Withers, C. Hoare).
5. **People first.** Gavin de Becker leads with the founder's portrait and credentials, then people cards. Withers and C. Hoare route to "Our People". Supports the founder-as-brand decision.
6. **Audience routing by situation, not product.** Withers hero names three audiences (individuals and families, founders and businesses, institutions). C. Hoare's "Supporting You" lists family offices, entrepreneurs, estates. Pictet offers a three-way profile selector. Fits Toza's situation landing pages.
7. **Plain contact block with a human phone number.** C. Hoare (phone and email near the top), Withers (phone plus enquiry link), Fortalice and USI (phone in footer). Lombard Odier routes via form by intent. For Toza, WhatsApp/Signal/call-back replaces the form.
8. **Heavy legal and regulatory footers as a trust signal.** FSCS and registration (Coutts), risk disclaimers (Lombard Odier), state licence numbers (Gavin de Becker). Toza analogue: a short, plain "what we do not do" line (no monitoring, no forensics, no legal advice).
9. **Generous whitespace, few sections.** Hoare, Pictet and Choate keep to roughly 5 to 10 blocks; vendors run 12 to 15.
10. **Thought-leadership as quiet authority**, not sales content (Hoare's letters, Withers and Pictet insights). Toza should keep this to a couple of founder-signed notes, if any.

### Borrow / avoid
- **BlackCloak:** borrow the private-client/enterprise audience split (separate Toza home vs situation pages); avoid press row, testimonials, awards, stat wall, demo pop-up.
- **USI:** borrow visible tiers (but show prices); avoid the "Inquire for Pricing" dead end and 24/7 monitoring language.
- **Fortalice:** borrow "discretion and clarity" tone only; avoid the "As Seen In" strip and testimonial block, which are banned for Toza.
- **Lombard Odier:** borrow lowercase calm labels and soft links; avoid the long intent-and-country form and cookie wall.
- **Pictet:** borrow one reflective line per screen and a profile selector; avoid the stacked region and location overlays.
- **Coutts:** borrow the clipped headline cadence and visible regulatory identity; avoid the awards list and repeating "Join" CTA.
- **C. Hoare:** borrow phone number and email near the top, plain "Supporting You" audience list; avoid the carousel (movement undermines calm).
- **Gavin de Becker:** borrow founder-first hero; avoid the many-headshot card carousel (Toza has one expert) and the client slider.
- **Withers:** borrow naming people in situations (individuals, families, founders) in the hero; avoid the gamified feature and pop-up language prompt.
- **Choate:** borrow the minimal structure; avoid the "click Agree" disclaimer gate (friction).

## 3. Implications for Toza Home and situation landing pages

1. Home: five to six screens only. Hero (one sentence, who Dr. Tabansky is, what the client gets), the ladder with prices, how a visit works, what is not done, contact.
2. Hero shows the founder's name and a plain description of work; no category label like "platform". Credentials via one link to his personal site, as Gavin de Becker and Hoare lean on named people and history.
3. Headline cadence: short fragments or one calm sentence per screen; no statistics, no threat verbs.
4. Show the three prices on the home page as a quiet table: ₪3,600 incl. VAT, ₪14,000, from ₪42,000. None of the 10 sites do this, so it signals confidence.
5. Put "what is included" and "conditions" (client present, client holds credentials, no monitoring, forensics or legal advice) one click away, banks' footer-disclosure style, but plain language.
6. One CTA only: WhatsApp / Signal / call-back, with a human phone line visible in the first screen (C. Hoare pattern). No form, no demo wording.
7. Situation landing pages (separation, founder dispute, inheritance, after private information surfaced) follow Withers/C. Hoare: named situation as heading, three short paragraphs, the ladder, contact. Address attorneys via a separate "for advisers" page, as Coutts and Withers separate audiences.
8. Proof by process: describe the dated record, hardware keys and written summary, as proof of method instead of logos.
9. No carousels, pop-ups, cookie walls, or auto-playing video; no rotating hero.
10. Imagery: restrained, human, rarely devices; a single portrait of the founder; no dashboards.

## 4. Anti-patterns in the cyber-vendor group

- Press-logo strips and "As Seen In" (BlackCloak, Fortalice).
- Testimonial carousels and award badge walls (BlackCloak, Fortalice).
- Stat-driven fear openers with cited percentages (BlackCloak).
- "Platform", "SOC", "24/7" and dashboard vocabulary (BlackCloak, USI).
- Demo or "Speak With An Expert" button repeated in every section.
- Tier cards that withhold prices ("Inquire for Pricing", USI).
- Pop-ups over the page (BlackCloak).
- Media/webinar promo banners above the nav.
- Stock executive-at-laptop photography (USI hero; photos inferred from description).
