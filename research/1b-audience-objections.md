# Toza v2: audience and objections research

Status: hypothesis document. Facts come from the repo (llms.txt, Editorial Doctrine, Copy Spec v3, _site/en pages). All buyer psychology, behaviour and Israeli-market claims are **[H] hypothesis** from desk reasoning; no external sources were used, so none are cited. Fact base: Assessment ₪3,600 incl. VAT (credited in full to a service started within 14 days); Personal Shield ₪14,000; Inner Circle from ₪42,000; urgent surcharge ₪4,800 (₪6,000 weekend/holiday); no testimonials or press; in-person only; no evidence collection, investigation, legal advice or remote control.

## 1. Personas [all H]


### P1. Spouse entering separation (the "Separating Partner")
- **Situation and trigger:** Separation decided or imminent, often after a lawyer is retained. Trigger: the other spouse quotes something private, or the shared Apple/Google family set-up suddenly feels like a liability.
- **Blind spot:** Recovery emails and phone numbers, family sharing, location sharing, shared cloud albums, the spouse-run home router.
- **Emotion:** Tense, ashamed of the topic, afraid of escalation and of looking paranoid.
- **Influencers:** Divorce lawyer (strong), a close friend or sibling, sometimes a new partner.
- **First look:** Phone, late evening or night, private browsing, possibly on a device the spouse can reach. Likely arrives via a lawyer, a Google search or a friend's recommendation.
- **Language:** Hebrew first. Some secular upper-middle-class readers use English.
- **"Premium" means:** Discretion, one person who has seen this before, nobody else in the loop, no paper trail on shared devices.
- **Contact today if:** She sees the exact situation described, a clear statement that she controls what changes and when, a neutral WhatsApp opener, and a price she can pay privately.
- **Leaves if:** Language implies spying or accusation. Any hint the work "fights" the spouse.

### P2. Founder in a partner dispute
- **Situation and trigger:** Co-founder or investor relationship breaking down. Trigger: a leaked plan, or a partner who still holds admin or recovery rights.
- **Blind spot:** The personal-to-business bridge: a personal email recovers the business account, one phone authenticates both, an old shared folder.
- **Emotion:** Controlled, analytical, irritated at being exposed.
- **Influencers:** Commercial lawyer (strong), a trusted board or co-investor, sometimes the company IT or CISO (a potential blocker).
- **First look:** Phone between meetings or at night, then desktop for due diligence on the person.
- **Language:** Hebrew and English both work. English-language startup culture reads English fluently.
- **"Premium" means:** Senior expert only, no handoff, scoped work, a tight start-to-finish time, clear personal-versus-company boundary.
- **Contact today if:** Page names the personal-business overlap precisely, states company systems are out of scope without authority, and offers a fast first reply.
- **Leaves if:** Sounds like enterprise security selling or an IT contractor. Vague scope.

### P3. Heir in an inheritance conflict
- **Situation and trigger:** Sibling or relative dispute over an estate. Trigger: a relative who helped with accounts, devices or cloud folders in good faith, now adversarial.
- **Blind spot:** The "helper" has persistent access (family recovery contact, shared cloud, the parent's old tablet).
- **Emotion:** Hurt, defensive, reluctant to treat family as adversaries.
- **Influencers:** Estate or inheritance lawyer, a spouse, sometimes the other siblings (who must not know).
- **First look:** Phone in the evening, often on family WhatsApp-connected devices.
- **Language:** Hebrew first. Possibly older (50s to 70s), where trust in a named person weighs more than design.
- **"Premium" means:** Dignity, no accusation of relatives, a calm adult tone.
- **Contact today if:** The page validates ordinary good-faith access, with no implication that family did anything wrong.
- **Leaves if:** Accusatory tone. Anything that sounds like surveillance of relatives.

### P4. Executive after a leak ("They know something")
- **Situation and trigger:** Someone quotes a private conversation or plan. Source unknown.
- **Blind spot:** Assumes a hack or a person.
- **Emotion:** Anxious, suspicious, wants an answer ("who did it").
- **Influencers:** Lawyer, board or chair, a security contact, a spouse.
- **First look:** Phone, any hour, sometimes in a panic. Searches symptoms ("someone knows my messages").
- **Language:** Hebrew, with some English.
- **"Premium" means:** Fast, senior, unflappable, honest about limits.
- **Contact today if:** Page says "you need not prove how it happened", offers an urgent route and states what the visit can and cannot tell him.
- **Leaves if:** Overpromising, or the opposite: no sense of speed. Disappointed when "no attribution" is revealed late.

### P5. Proactive affluent family ("Quiet Preparer")
- **Situation and trigger:** No dispute. Trigger: a peer's divorce or breach, a wealth event (exit, sale, inheritance), a child leaving home, a business transition.
- **Blind spot:** Four to eight devices of mixed hygiene, a shared router, recovery chains through a spouse or child, a household assistant with access.
- **Emotion:** Calm, comparison-shopping, can wait.
- **Influencers:** Spouse (co-decider), family office or wealth adviser, a friend who recommended.
- **First look:** Desktop or tablet, daytime or weekend, may read slowly and revisit.
- **Language:** Hebrew or English. Bilingual families.
- **"Premium" means:** Private-bank register: named individual, fixed scope, a record, no upsell pressure.
- **Contact today if:** Inner Circle logic is clear (four people, eight devices, eight keys, one network) and the scope table is exact.
- **Leaves if:** Fear marketing. Sounds like consumer antivirus.

### P6. The attorney referrer
Not a buyer; the highest-trust channel for P1 to P4. See section 3.

## 3. Attorney-referrer specifics [all H]

**Who:** Family, commercial/shareholder and estate attorneys, small and boutique firms. They refer when a client says "I think they see my messages".

**What they need before referring (hypothesis):**
1. **Confidentiality both ways.** No findings flow to the attorney without client authorisation. The attorney must not be exposed to client data inadvertently.
2. **Clear boundaries.** Not forensic, no chain of custody, not evidence, not legal advice. The attorney keeps the legal strategy. This protects the attorney from a tainted-evidence or professional-liability problem.
3. **No unlawful access.** Written confirmation that work is limited to the client's own authorised environment, so referral cannot be read as sanctioning surveillance of the other side.
4. **Predictable timing and coordination.** Ability to agree when changes happen relative to filings, negotiations or hearings, with client consent.
5. **Fee clarity.** Client pays directly. Avoid any referral fee (attorney ethics and optics; validate with Israeli counsel).

**Collateral an attorney would use (H):**
- A one-page "Referral note" in Hebrew and English: what Toza does, what it does not, what the client authorises, price, how to start.
- A neutral WhatsApp or Signal opener the lawyer can paste ("a possible referral").
- A sample authorisation template for client-approved coordination.
- A fictional, labelled sample attorney-friendly summary showing format and limits.

**Page implications:** /en/attorneys.html is sound on boundaries. Add a downloadable one-pager, a sample summary and a Hebrew version, and address the attorney's own risk before the client's benefit.

## 4. Decision triggers, timing and page architecture [all H]

**Trigger types**
- **Acute (P4, some P1, P2):** a quoted message, a change in the other side's behaviour, a hearing or negotiation date. Decision window hours to days. They search symptoms, not Toza. Need fast reassurance, a clear urgent route and a low-friction first message.
- **Event-driven (P1, P3):** lawyer retained, separation decided, estate opened. Window days to weeks. Strongly influenced by the lawyer.
- **Proactive (P5):** wealth event, a peer's incident. Window weeks to months. Reads more, compares, consults a spouse.

**Architecture implications**
1. **Enter by situation, not product.** Four short situation pages, each ending in one private contact action, plus Hebrew mirrors.
2. **Mobile-first and discreet.** Single-thumb layout, no autoplay or pop-ups, neutral tab title, fast on poor data.
3. **Price before contact.** The free qualifying chat is visible everywhere; price precedes it so objections are answered, not discovered.
4. **"Before you message" panel:** Signal option, neutral wording, shared-device caution (objection 11).
5. **Urgent lane:** one calm route with plain terms. **Proactive lane:** Inner Circle page with a spouse-forwardable scope table. **Attorney lane:** standalone with downloadable collateral.
6. **No scarcity or fear imagery; proof without testimonials.** Labelled method illustrations, operating facts, boundaries and the expert's verifiable background.
7. **Return visits:** stable URL and a short summary the prospect can send to themselves.

## 5. Owner interview script: 20 questions for Dr. Tabansky

Aim: validate or refute the hypotheses with anonymised experience; record no identifying detail.

**A. Who comes and why**
1. In the last ten clients, how did each first reach you (lawyer, friend, search, other)? What did they type or say first?
2. Which situation was most common: separation, business, inheritance, leak, proactive? Roughly what percentage each?
3. What was the actual trigger in the week before contact? Was it usually a specific event?
4. What did clients believe was happening versus what you found? Where were they most wrong?
5. What time of day and week do first messages arrive? Are they in Hebrew or English?

**B. Objections**
6. What do people ask before booking?
7. How often does price come up, when, and what do people compare it with? Have you lost people at ₪3,600, and at ₪14,000?
8. Do clients ask "is this legal" or "will it make things worse"? How do you answer?
9. How often do clients worry that the other side will notice, and has that actually happened?
10. Do clients bring up their own IT person or company IT? How does that conversation go?
11. How many clients worry about trusting one person? What reassures them in practice?
12. Do people trust a Hebrew or an English message more? Does the language of the site matter to them?

**C. Decision dynamics**
13. Who else is involved in the decision (lawyer, spouse, adviser)?
14. How long from first message to paid booking? What speeds it up, and what makes people vanish?
15. Which clients come urgently? Did the urgent surcharge cause friction, and is it used?
16. What do clients value most afterwards (the map, the changes, the teaching, the record)? What do they say when they recommend you?

**D. Attorneys and boundaries**
17. Which attorneys refer, in what types of cases? What did they ask you before referring? Do any ask for a fee or a report?
18. Have you ever had to decline a client because the request crossed a boundary (evidence, spying, investigating someone)?
19. What would make a lawyer feel safe sending you a client, and what has made one hesitate?

**E. Proof and disappointment**
20. When has a client been disappointed (no attribution, nothing found, change noticed)? What was unclear in advance? Which verifiable credentials (publications, roles, public record) can we show without breaching client privacy?

**Method:** tag each answer frequent/occasional/rare/never and by persona; re-rank section 2 before v2 copy locks.
