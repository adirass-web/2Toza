# Private information can be exposed without a hack.

Existing access, copies, shared information and data traces from everyday services reveal more than you intended. This is how others gain leverage over your money, reputation and decisions.

## Access that outlasts its purpose.

A calendar shared during a relationship, a folder used for family finances or an account still connected to a shared device continues to reveal information after circumstances change. The access may have been useful when it was set up. Its consequences deserve another look during a separation, inheritance conflict or business dispute.

## Copies outside the account you are checking.

A private document may also exist in a downloaded file, a forwarded message or a shared folder. Checking the original account alone does not tell you where its contents have gone. Understanding exposure means considering those copies as well as current access.

## What separate details reveal together. {#what-apps-reveal}

### What the phone shows {#exposure-traces}

- **Waze** Three drives to the same unfamiliar neighborhood.
- **Instagram** Furniture ads.
- **Call log** Two calls to a moving company.

### What someone might infer {#exposure-inference}

**You might be planning a move.** Someone who can see those records could put them together and make that guess.

The conversation with the mover is the content. The number you called, when you called, and how long you spoke are **metadata**. Those details can give someone a clue even if the conversation itself stays private. Trip times and destinations can add context. The furniture ads are a weaker clue and can appear for many reasons.

## Defaults do not fit your circumstances.

Default settings were not designed around the particular people, information and consequences that matter to you.

You can examine, harden and upgrade those arrangements before a known incident makes the risk urgent. Protect your private affairs proactively.

## Think of one thing you want to keep private.

It could be something that already happened or something you’re planning.

- What would change if it became known?
- Who would want to know, and why?
- Who can still see information connected to it?
- Where might copies already exist?
- What could someone work out by putting those details together?
