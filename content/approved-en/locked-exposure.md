# Private information can be exposed without a hack.

Existing access, copies, shared information and data traces from everyday services reveal more than you intended. This is how others gain leverage over your money, reputation and decisions. We have seen this firsthand everywhere.

## Access that outlasts its purpose.

A calendar shared during a relationship, a folder used for family finances or an account still connected to a shared device continues to reveal information after circumstances change. The access may have been useful when it was set up. Its consequences deserve another look during a separation, inheritance conflict or business dispute.

## Copies outside the account you are checking.

A private document may also exist in a downloaded file, a forwarded message or a shared folder. Checking the original account alone does not tell you where its contents have gone. Understanding exposure means considering those copies as well as current access.

## What separate details reveal together.

An illustrative example: a calendar entry shows a meeting, while a shared document identifies a financial matter. Together, those details may reveal whom you are consulting and what you are preparing to do. An opponent may use that knowledge in a negotiation or to anticipate a decision you intended to make privately.

Other exposed information can concern assets, private relationships or unshared plans. Its consequences may reach your money, reputation and freedom to decide.
